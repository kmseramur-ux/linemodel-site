A play needs the model's raw number to give its side at least a 57% chance of covering the market line, counting how often NFL margins land on 3 and 7, and that side must not be the more injured team or coming off a 17-point loss. The percentage shown is calibrated: it is how often plays that looked like this actually covered, which is lower than the raw figure because the model's disagreements with the market are partly noise. Anything below the threshold was a coin flip or worse in the backtest, so it is not shown as a lean. Market is the consensus line; Kalshi is the live line implied by its spread ladder. Best price is the highest expected value for the side across every number Kalshi lists within 3 points of the market, fees included, if the model's margin is right. The Season view shows how often it has been. Expect swings: at a 54% rate, a six-play week goes 0-6 about once in a hundred and 1-5 about one week in twelve, and the backtest's weekly results are no more clustered than independent bets, so a winless week is variance, not a signal to change the rule.

One row per number Kalshi lists within 3 points of the market for the model's side, plus the model's side of the winner market, priced at the ask with the taker fee. EV and stake use the model's raw probability, which overstates its real edge: plays that pass the card's rule have covered about 54% of the time, not the 56 to 62% shown here. Rank by the EV column, but size bets off the calibrated rate on the Card, and treat rows without a PLAY call as pricing information rather than bets. Deep alternates are excluded because the margin model is not calibrated in the tails. "Money with" is the share of Kalshi's pregame taker dollars that went to this side of the market, the moneyline or the spread numbers within 3 points: takers are the orders that crossed the spread, so it is money arriving rather than money waiting. Add sportsbook CSVs to shop.py and they appear here beside Kalshi.

Model

what it is and how it has tested

Power ratings from free play-by-play: Elo, opponent-adjusted EPA and success rate, the starting quarterback's prior efficiency, rest and situation. A tree model and a ridge regression are averaged. Every number for a game uses only games finished before that week. The backtest trains on every prior season and predicts the next, 2015 to 2026, graded against the closing spread.
